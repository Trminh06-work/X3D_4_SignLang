\paragraph{How many blocks should be frozen?} As can be seen in \textbf{Tab. \ref{tab:val_sweep}}, freezing from 0 to 2 blocks giving roughly equivalent validation accuracy, and undoubtedly freezing more blocks cost less than fewer blocks.
Meanwhile, training the classfier head alone does not offer competitive performance, similarly, letting the adjancent block to be trained give much higher accuracy, though not as well as unfreeze more blocks.
Therefore, we highly recommend enabling training 3 to 4 blocks, to maximise the models' learning capacity while maintaing computing efficiency.


\paragraph{Underfitting and overfitting.} The two failure modes occur at opposite ends of the sweep, and \textbf{Table \ref{tab:val_sweep}} separates them.
Training the classifier head alone underfits: those runs reach only $48.0\%$, $64.3\%$ and $69.8\%$ accuracy on the split they are trained on, for \texttt{X3D\_XS}, \texttt{X3D\_S} and \texttt{X3D\_M} respectively, whereas every run with at least one block unfrozen exceeds $92\%$.
A model that cannot fit half of its own training data is not overfitting, and the limitation is therefore representational rather than statistical, i.e. frozen \texttt{Kinetics--400} features are not separable into $226$ signs by a linear map.
This is not a matter of head capacity either, since the head still holds $1.43$M of the $3.44$M parameters, as reported in \textbf{Table \ref{tab:main_results}}.
Overfitting instead appears once the backbone is adapted, as a wide but stable gap between the two columns, e.g. $31.5$ points for \texttt{X3D\_XS} and $13.0$ points for \texttt{X3D\_M} with no block frozen.
The gap widens with freezing depth, reaching $43.2$ points for \texttt{X3D\_XS} at four frozen blocks, as the trainable remainder memorises the training split without gaining anything transferable.

% Accuracy only begins to fall from three blocks onwards, and does so sharply thereafter.

\paragraph{Clip sampling outweighs adaptation depth.} The ordering $\texttt{X3D\_M} > \texttt{X3D\_S} > \texttt{X3D\_XS}$ holds at every freezing depth, and the margin is large.
More specifically, \texttt{X3D\_M} with three blocks frozen ($83.4\%$) still exceeds fully fine--tuned \texttt{X3D\_S} ($81.5\%$), which in turn exceeds \texttt{X3D\_XS} by some seventeen points.
It is worth noting that the three variants share one architecture and one parameter count, as \textbf{Table \ref{tab:main_results}} confirms, and differ only in the clip drawn from each video.
The gain is thus attributable to temporal and spatial sampling rather than to capacity, and it is bought with computation at inference, i.e. $0.63$, $2.03$ and $4.90$ GFLOPs per clip.
The four--frame clip of \texttt{X3D\_XS} is the more plausible culprit, since a sign is a trajectory and four samples over roughly two seconds discard most of it.

\paragraph{Recommendation.} From the above–mentioned results and analysis, we shall pick \texttt{X3D\_M} with 2 frozen blocks as our ``best'' model, due to its performance competency.

\paragraph{Freezing depth.} Training the whole network rather than the head alone adds $39.4$, $46.3$ and $47.5$ top--1 points for \texttt{X3D\_XS}, \texttt{X3D\_S} and \texttt{X3D\_M}, at $2.4\times$ the trainable parameters.
The return is heavily front--loaded: unfreezing one further block, i.e. \texttt{res}$_5$, already recovers most of it, lifting \texttt{X3D\_M} from $34.86\%$ to $73.62\%$ for $1.35$M extra trainable parameters.
Thereafter the curve flattens, and the best test result is reached at one frozen block for both \texttt{X3D\_XS} and \texttt{X3D\_S}.

\paragraph{Model size.} Moving from \texttt{X3D\_XS} to \texttt{X3D\_S} costs $3.2\times$ the GFLOPs and returns $19.7$ top--1 points; the further step to \texttt{X3D\_M} costs $2.4\times$ again for only $5.8$.
The longer clip is therefore not wasted on isolated signs, but its value saturates.

\paragraph{Error type.} The top--1 to top--3 margin is widest for \texttt{X3D\_XS} ($17.7$ points at one frozen block) and narrowest for \texttt{X3D\_M} ($12.2$ at two).
Following \textbf{Sec. \ref{sec:metrics}}, the small variant's errors are largely near misses among confusable signs, whereas the residual errors of \texttt{X3D\_M} are more often outright failures.

\subsubsection{Generalising to our data}

This section is separated from the above–testing section. This serves to showcase the generalisation of our ``best'' \texttt{X3D} variant, as we believe the test set still shares some underlying patterns with the train set, e.g. personal identity, environment background and experimental bias.
To build this dataset manually, we shall rely on the \texttt{id} of the public dataset, watch their videos, attempt to replicate their motions and eventually annotate using the same \texttt{id}, as our model learnt from the numerically indexed classes, not text–based classes.

More specifically, this hand–crafted dataset poses some problems which we note the most apparent herein.
First, sign language may involve mouth movement, and thus, we are unable to mimic their mouth, which may diverge from what the models are trained on.
Second, the lighting conditions are poor, which may introduce blue and light scattering into the videos.
Third, in \texttt{AUTSL}, most people were recorded with their full body, whereas, in our hand–crafted data, we only recorded from the upper half of the body.

\paragraph{Setup.} We recorded ten clips on a laptop camera at $1620\times1080$, each imitating one \texttt{AUTSL} sign and named after the test clip whose label it borrows; the footage itself is our own.
They were converted to \texttt{mp4} and passed through the pipeline of \textbf{Sec. \ref{sec:experiment}} unchanged, i.e. uniform subsampling to $16$ frames at $224^2$, then classified by \texttt{X3D\_M} with two frozen blocks on a CPU.

\paragraph{Accuracy.} The model recognises six of the ten clips, i.e. $60\%$ top--1, $60\%$ top--3 and $70\%$ top--5, against $82.28\%$, $94.49\%$ and $96.52\%$ on the \texttt{AUTSL} test split.
Ten clips is far too small a sample to measure a gap, since one clip is worth ten points, but the direction is what the section set out to check and it is the expected one.

\paragraph{Error type.} The four errors are not near misses: three place the true sign outside the top five altogether, and only clip $4$ recovers it at rank five.
This is unlike the test split, where the top--1 to top--3 margin of $12.2$ points indicated confusion among similar signs.
Under a genuine change of signer, camera and background, the model fails outright rather than hesitating between candidates.

\paragraph{Confidence.} The wrong predictions are as confident as the right ones, i.e. $0.68$ to $0.97$ against $0.67$ to $1.00$.
Softmax confidence therefore offers no usable signal for rejecting an out--of--distribution clip.

\paragraph{Latency.} Inference took $0.293$ to $0.372$ seconds per clip, $0.325$ on average, on a CPU alone.
This is the wall--clock figure that GFLOPs cannot supply, as noted in \textbf{Sec. \ref{sec:metrics}}, and it suggests the variant is deployable at interactive speed without a GPU.
